# Prompting Projects

A strong project prompt defines the website. The OS should not have to infer the identity from generic words such as “modern” or “premium.”

## High-value controls

Include:

- audience and context;
- primary action;
- core promise;
- real proof and assets;
- emotional target;
- brand traits and anti-traits;
- reference sites or images and what you like about them;
- design mode;
- visual ambition;
- motion intensity;
- required functionality;
- content and legal constraints;
- target devices;
- available MCPs and licenses.

## Design modes

### Directed

Use when you have a visual target. Tell Cursor to implement it faithfully rather than creating unrelated alternatives.

### Explore

Use when you want to choose among visual ideas. Require compact routes with navigation, first screen, representative section, motion, and mobile state. Do not request three complete websites.

### Autonomous

Use when you want minimal manual labor. Require internal exploration and independent screenshot-first criticism before the agent selects one route.

## Avoid

- “Make it beautiful.”
- “Use every cool library.”
- a list of required concepts that implies one section per bullet;
- forcing a visual technique before the brand is understood;
- asking for complete strategy, copy, identity, implementation, and QA in one undifferentiated pass.
