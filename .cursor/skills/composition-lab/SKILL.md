# Composition Lab

Use this Skill to make a page feel designed rather than assembled.

## Navigation

Choose navigation based on the content and interaction model. Possibilities include:

- quiet inline navigation;
- floating pill or capsule;
- split brand and action navigation;
- transparent overlay that changes on scroll;
- sidebar or rail;
- editorial index;
- command-style navigation;
- conventional application shell.

None is the default. Test open, closed, hover, keyboard, mobile, and scrolled states.

## First screen

The first screen should establish:

- what the experience is;
- why it matters;
- where attention should move;
- what action is available;
- and what makes this project visually specific.

Do not fill empty space with unrelated floating UI.

## Background and depth

Select from the concept rather than habit:

- flat color;
- photography or video;
- illustration;
- typography-led composition;
- texture or grain;
- gradient or light field;
- shader or WebGL;
- grid, pattern, map, diagram, or data field;
- layered translucent surfaces;
- no decorative background at all.

Account for contrast, motion sensitivity, device capability, and content legibility.

## Section rhythm

Vary composition when the information changes. Possible structures include:

- full-bleed visual moments;
- narrow editorial text;
- product walkthroughs;
- side-by-side demonstrations;
- timelines;
- image galleries;
- split-screen stories;
- dense utility surfaces;
- intentionally simple pauses.

Avoid repeating one heading-plus-card-grid formula throughout the page.

## Output

Produce a concise composition map and implement enough of it to judge in the browser.
