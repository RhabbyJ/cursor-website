# Signature Surface Pass

Run after a visual route has been selected and before the complete site is expanded.

A concept route proves the art direction. It is not automatically production-ready.

## 1. Inventory the selected route

List every prominent surface:

- navigation;
- first-screen background;
- headline treatment;
- primary interaction;
- category/service/feature system;
- project or product showcase;
- buttons and calls to action;
- icons and illustration;
- section transitions;
- footer or final conversion surface.

Classify each as:

- **signature:** materially affects memorability;
- **supporting:** important but subordinate;
- **utility:** should remain simple and predictable.

Record this in `website-os/work/surface-inventory.md`.

## 2. Detect generic fallback

Flag surfaces that rely primarily on:

- identical bordered cards;
- repeated rounded rectangles;
- default icon-plus-heading-plus-copy blocks;
- generic radial gradients and blurred blobs;
- fake dashboards created only to occupy space;
- the same hover lift or fade-up everywhere;
- desktop hover behavior with no touch equivalent;
- visually weak placeholders treated as finished art.

Do not merely make generic cards shinier. Ask whether the information should be cards at all.

Consider alternatives such as:

- animated editorial lists;
- stacked or expanding panels;
- spatial maps;
- image-led tiles;
- focus or spotlight systems;
- typographic indexes with preview media;
- draggable or scroll-linked collections;
- layered scenes;
- responsive bento systems;
- full-width sequences;
- custom diagrams or demonstrations.

## 3. Source-first upgrade

For every signature surface that fails the generic check, invoke `/visual-source-scout`.

At high visual ambition, the selected direction should normally contain:

- at least one source-backed or bespoke hero/background primitive;
- at least one source-backed or bespoke repeated-content system;
- at least one carefully designed motion pattern beyond simple entrance animation.

These are quality expectations, not a requirement to use three unrelated libraries.

## 4. Adapt into one language

The final route must feel art-directed, not assembled from demos.

Unify:

- type;
- color and material;
- depth;
- border/radius logic;
- icon and illustration character;
- motion timing;
- responsive behavior;
- content density.

## 5. Browser judge

Render desktop, mobile, touch-equivalent, hover, focus, and reduced-motion states.

The visual critic should answer:

- Which surface is memorable?
- Which still looks generated from generic CSS?
- Does each repeated surface have a project-specific interaction or composition?
- Is the external source still recognizable as a pasted demo, or has it become part of the brand?
- Is the mobile treatment designed rather than merely stacked?

Do not expand the complete site until the selected route passes this review.
